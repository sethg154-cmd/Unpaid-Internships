Who can afford to work for free?

Unpaid internships ask students to contribute money and time before their career has even begun.

Explore the costs

Context

The offer comes with a cost.

These are categories to ask about. They are not a calculated budget.

Transport

Getting to the workplace can add a regular cost, even when a student lives at home.

Meals

Food during the workday may become another expense. Some employers reimburse it.

Housing

Moving for a role can mean taking on rent before earning anything from the internship.

Time

Internship hours may compete with paid work, classes and other commitments.

What these accounts show

Two individual experiences in India, from interview notes supplied by Ganeev Sethi. Exact costs and working hours were not collected. These accounts illustrate experiences and cannot estimate how common they are. Quotes retain the interviewees’ supplied wording.

The turn

Living at home does not make unpaid work free.

A local role can still require commuting money and time away from a paid job. Family support may make an offer possible for one student while another has to pass.

Employers decide whether roles are paid, what hours they require and which expenses they cover.

The ask

Make experience affordable.

Employers should offer paid internships and make the expectations clear before students commit.

Before advertising your next internship

  1. Budget for intern pay.
  2. Publish expected weekly hours and overtime expectations.
  3. State which expenses you cover and how reimbursement works.

This checklist is for reviewing a role. Checking an item records no public pledge and sends no information.
